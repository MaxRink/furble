# Semtech SX1262

**Part and package.** Semtech SX1262, a sub-GHz LoRa transceiver, +22 dBm
variant. The package is a QFN with an exposed pad; the pad count and size were
not read and are UNVERIFIED.

**Status.** future. `lib/hal/SX1262.cpp` is a pure driver and no shipped board
carries the part: the only caller in the tree is `tests/host/hal_drivers_test.cpp`,
and `plans/implemented/322-hardware-abstraction.md:38` names the T-Beam, the Pager, the Watch
Ultra and the Cardputer Cap as the boards that will need it.

**Role in furble.** furble has no use for the radio, but a board that carries
one must hold it in its lowest state. Left alone it idles in STDBY_RC, and any
falling edge on NSS wakes it. The driver pulses the reset pin, waits for BUSY to
rise and then to fall, sends SetSleep and confirms the part is asleep
(`lib/hal/SX1262.h:7-56`).

**Documents.** [SX1262-DS]

### The window after the reset release

The driver waits for BUSY to rise before it waits for it to fall, on the reading
that the line is low in the moment between the NRESET release and the start of
the calibration. **That window is an inference, not a datasheet statement.** The
document says the reset is followed by calibration (8.1 page 53), that BUSY is
set high in STARTUP at power-up or after a reset and goes low as the radio
leaves Sleep (9.1 page 60, 8.3.1 page 55), and that BUSY is high during
calibration (13.1.12 page 80). It never states that the line is low after the
release of an awake part, and no table bounds how soon it rises.

The fix is safe either way, which is why the inference does not need settling
before the driver ships:

- If the line is low after the release, the rise wait is what stops a ready wait
  from passing at zero delay, and it costs at most the 1 ms budget.
- If the line is already high, the rise wait is satisfied by its first sample and
  never delays. The stuck-BUSY path in the host test measures exactly that, and
  spends 10,200 us rather than the 11,200 us an earlier version of the header
  claimed.

What would settle it is a scope on BUSY from the NRESET rising edge on a board
that carries the part, which is U-6 of plan 367.

The whole sequence is bounded at 16,700 us: 200 us of reset, 1,000 us waiting
for BUSY to rise, 10,000 us waiting for it to drop, 500 us of no-SPI settle and
5,000 us confirming sleep. Two of the five are driver choices with no tabulated
value behind them, the 1 ms rise wait and the 5 ms sleep confirmation, and the
host case `sx1262 header total bound is the documented 16,700 us` sums the
constants against that total.

The two paths that return before SetSleep do not spend the full total. A BUSY
that never rises spends the 200 us reset and the 1,000 us assert budget, 1,200
us. A BUSY stuck high spends the 200 us reset and the 10,000 us ready budget,
10,200 us, because the rise wait is already satisfied and never delays. The host
cases `sx1262 BUSY that never rises stops inside the assert budget` and
`sx1262 stuck BUSY stays inside the documented total` pin both sums.

## Error handling and the NSS rule

`sleep()` returns false when BUSY never rises, when BUSY never drops, when the
SPI write fails, or when BUSY is not high afterwards, and it sends no SetSleep in
the first three cases (`lib/hal/SX1262.h:101-111`, `lib/hal/SX1262.cpp:25-58`).

Any falling edge on NSS wakes the chip, so the board adapter must keep NSS high
through MCU light sleep and deep sleep (`lib/hal/SX1262.h:54-55`). An adapter
that drives NSS low in sleep undoes the whole sequence.

## Known limitations instead of errata

No Semtech errata document exists for the part. Section 15 Known Limitations,
pages 109 and 110, is the substitute and lists four cases: modulation quality at
500 kHz LoRa bandwidth with the workaround through bit 2 of register 0x0889, PA
clamping under antenna mismatch, the implicit header timeout, and inverted IQ.
A board that uses a 500 kHz bandwidth has to apply the workaround itself, because
the driver only sleeps the part.

## Where the code implements it

- `lib/hal/SX1262.cpp:25` `sleep()`: reset pulse, a BUSY-high poll with a 1 ms
  budget, a BUSY-low poll with a 10 ms budget, a two-byte SetSleep frame, the
  500 us settle, then a BUSY-high poll with a 5 ms budget, 16,700 us in the
  worst case.
- `lib/hal/SX1262.h:64-74`: the `Port` seam the board implements, so the
  driver holds no SDK include.
- `tests/host/hal_drivers_test.cpp`: pins the opcode, the sleepConfig byte, the
  wait order and both early-return spends.

`POLL_US` at `lib/hal/SX1262.h:99` is a driver choice, not a datasheet figure.
It sets how often the BUSY line is sampled inside the 1 ms rise budget and the
10 ms ready budget.

## Hardware constraints

The sleep sequence has two failure modes worth naming, because the driver has
no retry and no caller yet.

- `lib/hal/SX1262.cpp:37-44` returns false when BUSY never rises inside the
  1 ms budget or never drops inside the 10 ms budget, before any SetSleep is
  sent. The radio then stays in STDBY_RC, which draws 0.6 mA (Table 3-5,
  page 17) instead of the 160 nA sleep state, so a board that carries the radio
  must retry the sequence or hold NRESET low until it does.
- `lib/hal/SX1262.cpp:57` waits for BUSY high inside the 5 ms budget and
  returns false on a timeout, so a caller can tell "asleep" from "the command
  went out but the chip did not confirm".
- The driver is unconnected: no file under `src/`, `include/` or `components/`
  includes `SX1262.h` at this commit, so no board misses a retry today.

## Facts still unverified

- The sleepConfig bit encoding, Table 13-2. The 0x00 value is CITED from the
  driver header; the opcode and the one-byte form are now read on page 72.
- The 0.6 mA standby and 160 nA sleep figures, which are CITED from Table 3-5
  without reading the table.
- A reset-to-ready figure. The document has none, so the 10 ms budget rests on
  Table 8-2, page 56, which is about SLEEP to STDBY_RC and not about reset, and
  the 1 ms rise budget is a driver choice. A scope on BUSY from the NRESET
  rising edge is what would size both.
- Whether BUSY is low in the window between the NRESET release and the start of
  the calibration, see [the window after the reset release](#the-window-after-the-reset-release).
- Externally supplied TCXO behaviour: the driver never configures it, and a
  board whose radio needs a TCXO would have to do that before the sleep call.
- An errata document. None was found, and section 15 Known Limitations is the
  substitute, see above.
