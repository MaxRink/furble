# Injoinic IP5306

**Part and package.** Injoinic IP5306, a power bank system-on-chip with a 2.1 A
charger, a 2.4 A boost and a state of charge indicator. Package and pinout were
not read and are UNVERIFIED.

**Status.** board support. M5Unified's `Power_Class` drives it on `m5stack-core`
and furble only reacts to its behaviour.

**Role in furble.** The power path on the M5Stack Core. The board support library
drives it, and furble only reacts to its behaviour: the Core has no reliable
software power-off, and the boost cuts the board when the load stays low
(`src/FurbleUI.cpp:11696`, `include/FurbleSettingsDefaults.h:15`).

**Documents.** [IP5306-REG], [IP5306-DS131], [IP5306-DS101]

The register document and the two datasheets are three different documents, not
three revisions of one. Only the register document defines the register map. Only
the datasheets carry the electrical tables. V1.01 and V1.31 differ in how they
number and name their sections, so a section number from one does not transfer
to the other: V1.01 has numbered sections and calls its electrical chapter 8,
while V1.31 has no numbered sections at all and no section 8 heading.

Row 10 is the one with practical consequences, and the vendor documents
contradict each other on it. The register document's second section, first item,
states that the standard IP5306 does not support I2C and that an I2C build is a
separate order, the IP5306_I2C model. Both datasheets mark the opposite in their
model selection tables: IP5306-DS131 page 3 of 11 and IP5306-DS101 section 5
page 3 of 10 both carry a tick in the I2C column for the plain IP5306 part
number, where the IP5303 and IP5305 rows carry a dash. The register document is
the later of the two (2017 against 2016) and the one that defines the protocol,
so it is the one quoted. The order code still has to be confirmed with the
vendor before a board is designed around the bus.

Every register in this page therefore describes a part whose I2C support the
vendor documents disagree about. A board that carries a part with no I2C answers
nothing at 0xEA.

Row 8 and row 9 together explain the battery level. The register list stops at
0x77, the chip measures no voltage or current of its own, and the vendor advises
an external ADC. M5Unified nonetheless reads 0x78 and decodes a percentage from
it. That register is not in the vendor list, so the level path stays UNVERIFIED
and rests on the pinned library alone.

### The boost bit, corroborated by the pinned library

Plan 385 checked the two registers `include/FurbleIP5306.h` names against the
pinned M5Unified 0.2.20 rather than only against the register document.
`IP5306_Class.cpp` line 28 names the boost-always-on select `BOOST_OUT_BIT` with
the value `0x02`, which is bit 1 of `SYS_CTL0` (0x00, declared at line 14), and
its `setPowerBoostKeepOn()` at lines 112 to 120 is a read-modify-write of
exactly that bit, which is what `holdBoostOn()` reads back. The `SYS_CTL2` light-load
field the header encodes is the register document's own table: 00 is 8 s, 01 is
32 s, 10 is 16 s and 11 is 64 s, so 11b is the longest window and
`withLongestLightLoad()` selects it. Nothing in the header disagrees with either
document.

## The light-load timeout in this firmware

- The 45 mA is the load condition of the Automatic load detection time row, in
  the V1.01 control system table on page 5 of 10 and in the V1.31 table on page
  6 of 11. Both give 32 s.
- The four selectable values come from the register document, not from either
  datasheet. Only 01b is 32 s.
- The pinned M5Unified 0.2.20 writes 0x02 = 0b01101100 for the Core at
  `Power_Class.cpp:617`, inside the `board_M5Stack` array written at `:648`. Bits
  3:2 are 11b, so the shipped value is 64 s.
- Three places in the tree state 32 s: `include/FurbleSettingsDefaults.h:15`,
  `docs/power/board-power.md:24` and `:113`, and `:162`. That is the 01b value,
  so those statements are wrong for this build. They are documentation errors,
  and this change does not edit them.

## The charge-status policy

- The chip reports charging on 0x70 bit 3 and a full charge on 0x71 bit 3.
- `src/FurbleBoard.cpp:43` gives the Core the capability mask
  `{true, false, false, false}`. The fields are level, voltage, current and
  charging (`include/FurblePowerBackend.h:26` to `:31`), so furble reports
  charging as unsupported on the Core.
- That is a firmware policy, not a chip limit. M5Unified's `isCharging()` reads
  the same bit at `IP5306_Class.cpp:106` to `:109`.

## Where the code implements it

- `src/FurbleUI.cpp:11696`: the power-off path note.
- `include/FurbleSettingsDefaults.h:15`: the low-load cut that sets a brightness
  and load floor on the Core.
- `src/FurbleBoard.cpp:9`: the battery reporting note for the Core.
- `tools/power-model/board-currents.yaml` `pmic` block: the standby, input

## Redistribution

Both Injoinic documents permit conditional reproduction, so the earlier claim
that every document in this corpus failed the redistribution test was wrong for
these two.

- IP5306-REG section 4, page 9: "对于英集芯的产品手册或数据表，仅在没有对内容进行任何篡改且带有相关授权、条件、限制和声明的情况下才允许进行复制。" That is, Injoinic's manuals and datasheets may be copied only without any alteration to the content and with the relevant authorisations, conditions, limitations and statements retained.
- IP5306-DS131 page 11, Important Notice: "Reproduction of significant portions of INJOINIC TECHNOLOGY's information in INJOINIC TECHNOLOGY's data books or data sheets is permissible only if reproduction is without alteration and is accompanied by all associated warranties, conditions, limitations, and notices."

Both are conditional grants, which satisfies condition 1 of the rule in
[README.md](README.md#committed-documents). Condition 4 still blocks committing
these particular bytes: IP5306-REG comes from an M5Stack mirror and
IP5306-DS131 from a personal blog, and a document taken from a mirror is never
committed. The documents are cited only. No copy of either is in the tree.
